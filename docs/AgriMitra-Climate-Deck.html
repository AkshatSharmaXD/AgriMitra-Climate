01

The user

Ravi farms 0.8 hectares in Alwar.

He knows rain is coming. What he does not know is whether, given his loamy soil, his limited irrigation, the state of his wheat this week and the forecast, he should irrigate now or wait.

  • The nearest extension officer covers roughly a thousand farms like his.
  • Weather apps give a forecast, not a decision.
  • A disease scanner names a disease, then stops.
  • Advice that arrives on WhatsApp is generic to the district, not to the field.

Rain is coming. So — do I irrigate today, or lose the window?

Farmers who receive early, accurate guidance cut crop losses by 30–60%. The signal exists. It does not reach the field in a form anyone can act on.

03

Problem · why today's tools miss

Six apps, no answer.

The data a farmer needs already exists. It arrives in fragments, none of which knows about the others, and none of which knows the field.

Weather is not advice

A 7-day forecast is an input. Turning it into “irrigate Thursday, not today” needs the soil, the crop stage and the water available.

Diagnosis stops at the name

A scanner returns “Early blight” and ends. It never reaches the risk score, and never changes what the farmer is told to do next.

Satellite stays with agronomists

NDVI is routine in enterprise platforms and absent from the farmer's phone.

Advice is district-wide

Two farms in one district with different soil and irrigation get identical guidance.

Confidence is never shown

Tools present estimates as measurements. A farmer cannot tell which number to trust.

States cannot share models

Every state rebuilds its own crop dataset and risk logic. Nothing is interoperable.

The pattern

Each product owns one layer well and hands the farmer the integration problem.

The cost

Six apps, six logins — and still no answer for this field, this week.

The opening

Nobody is competing on the join between the layers.

04

The solution

One field. Five signals. One decision.

Deterministic logic computes the score. Gemini explains it. The model never invents a measurement — an architectural rule, enforced in code.

  1. 01Field profile — location, crop, season, soil, irrigation. Entered once; GPS fills district and state.
  2. 02Live weather — current conditions and a 7-day forecast for that coordinate.
  3. 03Satellite vegetation — Sentinel-2 NDVI reduced over the field via Google Earth Engine.
  4. 04Leaf scan — a photograph routed to MobileNetV2, escalating to Gemini multimodal below the confidence threshold.
  5. 05Risk score — five weighted factors, and a written note for every factor that had no evidence.
  6. 06Advisory — Gemini narrates the computed numbers in English, हिन्दी, ગુજરાતી or తెలుగు.

Risk engine

Water 0.30 · Heat 0.20 · Disease 0.20 · Rainfall 0.15 · Vegetation 0.15

When the weather service is unreachable, heat and rainfall are dropped from the weighted score and the remaining weights renormalised — never silently read as zero.

Shown to the farmer

“No leaf analysis on record; disease risk uses the neutral baseline (30/100). Scan a leaf to replace it.”

Deterministic first

The score is arithmetic over five named weights — reproducible and auditable.

Gemini second

The model narrates the computed numbers. It cannot change a score.

Farmer last

Plain language, four languages, every assumption written beneath the number.

05

What makes it different

It tells the farmer what it does not know.

Agricultural advice moves money and food. A confident wrong answer is worse than an admitted gap — so provenance is a first-class field in the API, not a footnote in the UI.

  • Every measurement carries is_live, degraded and is_demo — the types make them non-optional, so a screen cannot render a number without the means to label it.
  • Demo satellite data says “Demo dataset — not a live satellite observation.”
  • With no Gemini key, the advisory is labelled rule-based rather than passed off as model output.
  • Mandi prices return a 503 when unconfigured. Never an estimate.
  • The leaf scanner names the 14 crops it was trained on, and warns before you photograph a crop outside them.

Architecture

Google AI, load-bearing.

Each service below has a caller and a user-reachable code path. Where credentials are absent the feature degrades to a labelled fallback — it is never faked to pad a list.

Reasoning

Gemini API · Vertex AI

Advisory, assistant, district interventions, crop-ranking explanations. Structured output, temperature 0.2, and a system instruction forbidding invented measurements. Vertex gives a second auth path using service-account IAM, so Cloud Run holds no API key.

Vision

Gemini multimodal

Second opinion whenever the local classifier falls below 0.70 — which is every crop outside its 14 trained ones, i.e. most Indian staples.

Geospatial

Sentinel-2 NDVI reduced over a 150 m buffer, cloud-filtered to 30%. Maps Platform renders the district risk layer.

Cloud Speech-to-Text and Text-to-Speech for the browsers with no Web Speech API — Android WebView, most in-app browsers. Translation localises the scheme directory.

09

The thesis

A crop dataset is a public good. Ship it as one.

The challenge asks for an interoperable network that lets Indian states share agricultural data models. Two things in this system already are portable models — they simply need publishing as such.

  • The crop dataset — ten crops with season, soil, water, temperature and rainfall envelopes.
  • The risk model — five named weights, four bands, and the rules for dropping a factor with no evidence.

Published as versioned JSON Schema behind a federation API, Rajasthan's crop model becomes something Maharashtra can adopt, critique or replace — without a bilateral integration.

Federation, privacy first

  • Aggregates only. No farm record, no farmer record, no coordinate finer than a district centroid crosses a boundary.
  • A k-anonymity floor. A district with fewer than ten farms is omitted — “1 farm, CRITICAL, Wheat” identifies a person.
  • Signed manifests. A peer verifies before ingesting, so a hostile node cannot poison another state's dashboard.
  • Enforced by test, not by policy prose.

Open licence · open standard · documented · no PII · platform independent — the five Digital Public Good criteria, each mapped to where the code satisfies it.

Running end to end

  • Live Open-Meteo weather and measured seasonal rainfall from the historical archive.
  • Leaf scan: 20 ms rejection of non-leaves, 160–260 ms inference, 612 ms for a 3600 px photo.
  • District roll-up aggregating 100 farms into risk bands and dominant crops.

Bugs found and fixed by testing, not reading

  • A confident healthy leaf produced a disease risk of 95/100.
  • The leaf detector rejected every diseased colour — rust, blight, necrosis — as “not a plant”.
  • Crop rainfall was compared in mm/hour against a mm/season requirement, so that criterion never scored.
  • A dead weather API was read as “0 °C, no rain” instead of as missing.

Timing

Why this is buildable now and was not three years ago.

Three curves crossed. Satellite analysis became free at the field scale, multimodal reasoning became an API call, and the farmer's device became capable of both.

Then: enterprise only

Satellite went free and granular

Sentinel-2 revisits every five days at 10 m, and Earth Engine reduces it over a single field on demand. What needed an agronomy contract in 2020 is one API call per farm.

Then: bespoke models

Multimodal reasoning became callable

A leaf photograph plus structured farm context in one prompt, answered in Hindi or Gujarati, is a 2024-onwards capability. Before that it meant a trained model per crop per language.

Then: feature phones

The device caught up

Rural smartphone penetration now carries a camera good enough for lesion detection and a browser capable of speech input — which is what makes a farmer-facing product possible at all.

Impact · modelled, not measured

What one district would be worth.

The arithmetic below is a model built from the sourced figures on slide 3, not a result. It is shown so the assumptions can be argued with.

2,340farms in a district of Alwar's size
15–25%of yield currently lost to pests and disease
30–60%reduction where guidance arrives early and accurately
4.5–15%of yield recoverable, as the product of those ranges

How to read this

  • The 30–60% figure describes farmers who received early accurate guidance — it is not a claim about this product.
  • Reaching it requires the advisory to be both timely and correct, which is what the roadmap's remaining items address.
  • No revenue model is presented. A public good funded by a state department has different economics to a startup, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

What would have to be true

  • A disease model trained on Indian staples — wheat, rice, mustard, chickpea — which the current checkpoint has never seen.
  • Advisory quality validated against agronomists, not against a demo.
  • Enough farms per district to clear the k-anonymity floor before any aggregate is published.
  • An extension officer willing to keep one district's records current.
